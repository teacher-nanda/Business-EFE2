Finished, or in progress?

Click a sentence, then click the box where the highlighted verb belongs.

✅ Past simple
a completed action, a past habit, or a state that was true for a time

⏳ Past continuous
an action in progress at a certain time in the past

💭 Check your understanding

  • “I ran my own construction company.” Is the company still mine now? No. It is finished. We use the past simple for completed actions and states.
  • “I was working 60 hours a week before I came here.” Which verb is the long action in progress, and which is the short, finished action? Was working is the long action in progress. Came is the short, finished action (past simple).

How we build them

The past simple is usually formed by adding “-ed” to the base form of the verb.
The past continuous is formed by adding “was” or “were” in front of the verb, and “-ing” to the end of the verb.

📐 Grammar

A polite way to ask

You may hear people ask questions about a present situation in the past tense.
This makes the question more polite.
The past tense is also sometimes used to make a polite request.

Did you want a tour of the office?

“Do” becomes “Did” to make the question in past tense.

I wanted to ask about the company's history.

A polite request about something you want now.

💭 Check your understanding

  • Is the person asking about a tour in the past? No. The tour is for now. The past tense is just more polite.
  • Which sounds more polite: “Do you want a tour?” or “Did you want a tour?” “Did you want a tour?”

Workplace culture

Small talk, big benefits

What research says about quick chats with colleagues, baristas and strangers

When offices closed in 2020, millions of people lost something they had hardly noticed: the quick chat with the person at the next desk, the barista or a colleague in the elevator. Sociologists call these brief, low-stakes contacts “weak ties”. Researchers at Harvard worried about what would happen without them.

Sociologist Mario Small has studied how people talk about things that matter. He found that adults confide in someone they are not especially close to more than half of the time. Ashley Whillans, a professor at Harvard Business School, adds that casual work contacts are an underestimated source of creativity, and that they help people make sense of their jobs.

Many companies answered with more meetings, but Whillans says that planned video calls cannot replace a conversation in the hallway. Video calls are tiring, and many people have worked longer days, so they have no time left for informal chats. Her advice is to use a short message or a phone call instead of another long video meeting.

Newer research points in the same direction. Esra Ascigil and her colleagues surveyed thousands of people in Turkey and the United Kingdom. People who had started more conversations with strangers and acquaintances during the previous week were more satisfied with their lives. The surveys rely on what people remember, so the results are not perfect. Earlier experiments gave similar results: commuters in London who chatted with a stranger enjoyed the trip more, and coffee-shop customers who had a real exchange with the barista were in a better mood.

Sources: Harvard Gazette and Psychology Today.

⭐ Lesson Review

Lesson summary

⚙️

Past simple and past continuous

Past simple = a finished action.

I worked in a bank.

Past continuous = an action in progress.

I was working when she called.

Use both together:

I was driving when I saw this job advertised.

🗂️

Present perfect or past simple?

Present perfect = experience, no finished time.
Use for and since.

I have worked in a few teams.

Past simple = a finished time.

I worked in a small team in 2005.

🧩

Sounding polite

Use the past to sound polite and less direct.

Did you want some more coffee?

I was wondering if you could help me.

-ed endings have three sounds: /t/ worked, /d/ played, /ɪd/ wanted.
