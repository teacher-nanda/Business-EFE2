📐 Grammar

Finished, or in progress?

Talking about your past work experience is a good way to get to know your colleagues.
Click a sentence, then click the box where it belongs.

Past simple
a single, completed action, a past habit, or a state that was true for a time

Past continuous
an ongoing action that was in progress at a certain time in the past

💭 Check your understanding

  • “I ran my own construction company.” Is the company still mine now? No. It is finished. We use the past simple for completed actions and states.
  • “I was working 60 hours a week before I came here.” What happened next? The speaker came here. The long action (working) was in progress when the short action (came) happened.
📐 Grammar

How we build them

The past simple is usually formed by adding “-ed” to the base form of the verb.
The past continuous is formed by adding “was” or “were” in front of the verb, and “-ing” to the end of the verb.

Rest of clause60 hours a week
Past simplebefore I came here.

📐 Grammar

A polite way to ask

You may hear people ask questions about a present situation in the past tense.
This makes the question more polite.
The past tense is also sometimes used to make a polite request.

Did you want a tour of the office?

“Do” becomes “Did” to make the question in past tense.

I wanted to ask about the company's history.

A polite request about something you want now.

💭 Check your understanding

  • Is the person asking about a tour in the past? No. The tour is for now. The past tense is just more polite.
  • Which sounds more polite: “Do you want a tour?” or “Did you want a tour?” “Did you want a tour?”

📐 Grammar

Is there a date?

The present perfect simple is used to talk about events in the recent past that still have an effect on the present moment.
Click a sentence, then click the box where it belongs.

Present perfect
no date is specified

Past simple
a specific date is given

💭 Check your understanding

  • “I have worked in a few different teams.” Do we know when? No. No date is specified, so the present perfect is used.
  • “I worked in a small team in 2005.” Why the past simple? A specific date is given, so the past simple is used.

Workplace culture

The best meeting happens at the coffee machine

How a five-minute chat helped a new employee—and her whole team

When Elena Novak joined a logistics company in Rotterdam last spring, she did not know a single person on her floor. “I was eating lunch alone at my desk and I felt invisible,” she remembers. Three weeks later everything had changed, and it started at the coffee machine.

One morning, while Elena was waiting for her coffee, a colleague from the finance department asked a simple question: “What were you doing before you came here?” She had worked in three countries and had managed a small warehouse team in Poland. Her colleague had never met anyone with that experience, and soon half the floor was asking her questions.

Managers have started to notice the effect of these short chats. Many companies have created “conversation corners” instead of formal ice-breaker events, and some have changed the layout of their offices so that people from different departments meet by accident. Employees say they have learned more about each other’s skills in ten minutes of small talk than in a month of emails.

Elena has now worked at the company for a year. She has led two cross-department projects, and both started with a conversation over a cup of coffee. “I wasn’t looking for a project,” she says. “I was just asking a colleague about his weekend.”
