📐 Grammar

Always, or right now?

Present simple and present continuous

The present simple is used to describe something that happens in general, or is part of a routine.
The present continuous describes something that is happening right now, and will be continuing for a limited time.

I don't usually enjoy networking, but I'm enjoying this conference.
Present simple is the same as the base form of the verb without “to.”
Present continuous is formed by adding “be” before the verb and “-ing” to the verb.

Present simple

in general · part of a routine
I don't usually enjoy networking.

Present continuous

right now · for a limited time
I'm enjoying this conference.

💭 Check your understanding

  • Do I enjoy networking in general? No. “I don't usually enjoy networking” is about my general habit.
  • Is the conference happening now? Yes. “I'm enjoying this conference” is about now.
  • Will I enjoy this conference for ever? No. It is only for a limited time, because the conference will end.
📐 Grammar

How we build them

Present simple

PositiveI / you / we / they work
he / she / it works
NegativeWe don't work on Fridays.
She doesn't work in Finance.
QuestionDo you travel a lot?
Does he work here?

Present continuous

PositiveI'm working
you / we / they're working
he / she's working
NegativeI'm not working.
He isn't working. They aren't working.
QuestionIs she presenting today?
Are you staying here?

Verbs we don't usually use in the continuous

Some verbs describe a state, not an action.
We usually use the present simple, even for now.

knowlikelovehatewantneedunderstandbelieve

I know your manager.   I'm knowing your manager.

think and have change meaning:

I think it's a great venue. (opinion)    I'm thinking about the next talk. (an action in my head)

She has a small office. (possession)    We're having lunch. (an activity)

📐 Grammar

Small words that matter

I'd like to + verb
I'd like to introduce you to the new director.
I like this venue.

I'd like = I would like.
It is a polite way to say “I want to.”
Compare: I like = something I enjoy.

Nice / great / a pleasure + to + verb
Nice to meet you.
Great to see you again!
It's a pleasure to meet you.

After “to,” use the base verb.
The verb has no ending.

You must be…
You must be the new director.

You say “must be” when you are almost sure who someone is.

meet, met, have met
Did you meet Ana yesterday?
We met last year.
Have they met your manager?

did + base verb; the past form is “met”; have + met.

Checking with a question tag
I think we met in Mumbai, didn't we?
You're from Lima, aren't you?

The tag repeats the auxiliary: did→didn't we?   are→aren't you?

Career Ladder

Making connections

How to network better if you're shy

Networking doesn't necessarily mean talking to hundreds of people at a conference. A few good connections are much better than meeting lots of people who you will never hear from again. Start by chatting to ex-colleagues or old friends. Ask what they are doing now and share your experiences.

One common habit of shy people is to constantly apologize for everything. Apologizing all of the time looks unprofessional and shows a lack of confidence in yourself. Instead of saying sorry, remember to smile, maintain eye contact, ask questions, and, of course, exchange business cards.

Essential Skills

Meeting and greeting

Meeting new people isn't always easy, but it's an essential skill for a young business professional.

Whether you're looking for a new job, hope to grow your business, or just want to find new clients, you need to talk to the right people. It doesn't always lead to success, but it can provide a great first step. So, what's the best way to start talking?

Talk about your recent experience: “I'm working with some great software engineers at the moment” is a great way to start. You can tell them about your personal life and interests: “I play golf with my friend on the weekend” might be a good starter. But you shouldn't talk about things that are too personal. If you ask someone how much money they earn, they might be offended! Another good idea is to talk about one of your clients: “I often work with ElectroSan, an exciting new Japanese start-up.” You will soon find that the person you're talking to wants to know more…
